How to play

The numbers on the ledges

A ledge's number is what it costs in air to move onto it. They are rolled fresh every game: ledge 1 is always 1–2, the middle of a trench runs 2–5, and the bottom third runs 3–6 — the floor is never cheap. So each board is a different route to plan.

Paying for a move

You hold 5 air cards, refilled after every move from a deck shared by the whole table. To drop a diver one ledge, discard any combination summing to that ledge's number exactly — no overpaying. A ledge glows when your current hand can pay it. If nothing is payable, your turn is spent swapping the whole hand for a fresh one.

You hold 5 air cards drawn from your own supply of 12 — not a shared deck. To drop a diver one ledge, discard any combination summing to that ledge's number exactly — no overpaying. A ledge glows when your current hand can pay it.

If nothing is payable, your turn is spent surfacing for air: everything you have spent comes back, minus two cards burnt for good. Your supply only ever gets smaller. When it can no longer fill a hand you are out of air, and your divers are pulled out with nothing.

Forcing a ledge

If nothing in your hand pays a ledge exactly, you may push on: spend cards summing to more than its number and descend anyway. A push may never spend more cards than it needs — and everything it spends is burnt for good rather than discarded, so it never comes back to you.

The line — this is the game

Divers sharing a ledge are clipped to one line, in the order they arrived. When you descend, every diver that landed on that ledge after yours comes with you, free — and nobody who was already there.

So the diver that got there first tows everyone; the one that arrived last tows nobody. On the board they are drawn left to right in arrival order: the leftmost diver on a ledge is the one doing the towing.

Entering a trench always puts you on top of whoever is already on that ledge — so you tow nobody, and they tow you. That is the cheapest way down, if they keep moving.

Treasure, and running out of it

The first diver to a trench floor takes the richest token; later arrivals take what is left. When the last token goes, the trench is stripped — it closes, and every diver still inside surfaces with nothing, wasting every card spent getting there.

Winning

The game ends when someone surfaces their last diver; everyone gets one final turn. Divers still in the water score nothing. Most treasure wins.

Sunken Hold

A deep-sea salvage race

  1. Spend air cards to drop a diver one ledge. You must pay a ledge's number exactly.
  2. Divers sharing a ledge are clipped to one line — when you descend, everyone who landed there after you comes too, free. Arrive first and you tow them all; arrive last and you tow nobody.
  3. First to a wreck takes the richest treasure. When the last token goes, the trench is stripped and everyone still inside surfaces with nothing.
